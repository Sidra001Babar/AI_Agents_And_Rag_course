# Sequence Models — Historical Context

Sequence models are AI models that process data where order matters, such as text, speech, and time-series data.

Example: "I am not happy" has a different meaning from "I am happy not."

## 1. RNNs (Recurrent Neural Networks)
- RNNs process a sequence one item at a time.
- They maintain a hidden state that carries information from previous items.
- They can struggle to remember information from long sequences.

Example:
In "Ali went to school because he had an exam," an RNN tries to use earlier words to understand the sentence.

## 2. LSTMs (Long Short-Term Memory)
- LSTM is an improved type of RNN.
- It uses gates to control what information to keep, remove, or output.
- It can remember important information for longer sequences.

Example:
In a long paragraph, an LSTM may retain the subject's name to help interpret a later sentence.

## 3. GRUs (Gated Recurrent Units)
- GRU is another improved type of RNN.
- It uses gates to decide which information to keep or update.
- It has a simpler structure than an LSTM and can be faster to train.

Example:
A GRU can process a sentence while carrying useful information from earlier words.

**LSTM vs GRU:** Both help preserve information over time. GRUs are generally simpler, while LSTMs have a more detailed memory mechanism.

## 4. Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) Models
- Seq2Seq models convert one sequence into another sequence.
- They were commonly used for machine translation and text summarization.
- The input and output sequences can have different lengths.

Example:
Input: "Bonjour"
Output: "Hello"

## 5. Encoder-Decoder Architecture
This architecture is commonly used in Seq2Seq models.

- **Encoder:** Reads the input and creates a representation of its information.
- **Decoder:** Uses that representation to generate the output sequence.

Example (translation):
- Encoder reads: "I love learning."
- Decoder generates: "J'aime apprendre."

RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs, and Transformers can be used in encoder-decoder systems.

## 6. Vanishing-Gradient Problem
- During training, a neural network uses gradients to update its parameters.
- In long sequences, gradients can become extremely small as they move backward through many time steps.
- Earlier words then have little influence on learning, making long-term relationships difficult to learn.

Example:
In a long sentence, the model may struggle to connect a pronoun near the end with a name mentioned much earlier.

**Solution:** LSTMs and GRUs help reduce this problem, but they do not eliminate every long-range learning difficulty.

## 7. Why Attention Became Necessary
Traditional encoder-decoder models often compressed the entire input into one fixed-size representation. Important details could be lost, especially in long sequences.

**Attention allows a model to focus on the most relevant parts of the input when generating each output.**

Example:
When translating "The student who studied hard passed the exam," the model can focus on relevant words instead of relying only on a single summary of the sentence.

### Why attention was important
- Helps capture relationships between distant words.
- Makes better use of important input information.
- Improves many long-sequence tasks.
- Became a key component of Transformer models.

## Historical Progress

RNNs → LSTMs and GRUs → Seq2Seq with Attention → Transformers

**Remember:** RNNs process sequences step by step; LSTMs and GRUs improve memory; Seq2Seq maps one sequence to another; attention helps focus on relevant information; Transformers build on attention to model relationships between tokens efficiently.
