## 1. Text Preprocessing

### Definition
Text preprocessing is the process of cleaning and preparing raw text before providing it to an NLP model.

Raw text may contain uppercase letters, punctuation, extra spaces, spelling mistakes, and unnecessary words. Preprocessing helps make text more consistent and easier to process.

### Example

Original text:
"I LOVE NLP!!! I am learning NLP."

After preprocessing:
"i love nlp i am learning nlp"

### Common Text Preprocessing Techniques

#### A. Lowercasing
Converts all uppercase letters into lowercase.

Example:
- Before: "I LOVE AI"
- After: "i love ai"

Purpose: Helps treat uppercase and lowercase versions of a word consistently.

#### B. Removing Punctuation
Removes punctuation marks when they are not needed.

Example:
- Before: "Hello!!! How are you?"
- After: "Hello How are you"

Purpose: Reduces unnecessary symbols in text.

Note: Punctuation should not always be removed because it can carry meaning.

#### C. Removing Extra Whitespace
Removes unnecessary spaces between words.

Example:
- Before: "I   love    AI"
- After: "I love AI"

Purpose: Makes text consistent.

#### D. Tokenization
Divides text into smaller units called tokens.

Example:
- Before: "I love AI"
- After: ["I", "love", "AI"]

Purpose: Converts text into manageable units for NLP processing.

#### E. Stop-Word Removal
Removes common words that may not be useful for a particular task.

Example:
- Before: "I am learning the language"
- After: "learning language"

Common stop words include:
- is
- am
- the
- a
- an

Important: Stop words should not always be removed. For example, removing "not" from "I am not happy" changes the meaning.

#### F. Stemming
Reduces words to a shortened root form, which may not be a valid dictionary word.

Example:
- studies → studi
- playing → play

The exact result depends on the stemming algorithm.

#### G. Lemmatization
Converts words into their meaningful dictionary base forms.

Example:
- studies → study
- running → run
- better → good (depending on context and the lemmatizer)

Difference:
- Stemming may produce incomplete words.
- Lemmatization aims to produce valid dictionary forms.

### Real-World Example
A sentiment-analysis system analyzes customer reviews to determine whether they are positive or negative.

Original review:
"THE PRODUCT IS AMAZING!!!"

After preprocessing:
"the product is amazing"

The cleaned text can then be passed to a model for sentiment classification.

## 2. Characters, Words, Subwords, and Tokens

These are different ways of dividing text into smaller units.

Consider the word:

"playing"

### A. Characters

Definition:
Characters are individual letters, digits, spaces, or punctuation marks in text.

Example:
"cat" contains three characters:
- c
- a
- t

The word "playing" contains seven characters:
p, l, a, y, i, n, g

Uses:
- Spelling correction
- Character-level language models
- Detecting typing mistakes

### B. Words

Definition:
Words are language units that usually represent meaning and are separated according to word boundaries.

Example:
"I love NLP"

Words:
- I
- love
- NLP

Number of words: 3

Uses:
- Keyword extraction
- Word counting
- Basic text analysis

### C. Subwords

Definition:
Subwords are smaller units that represent parts of words.

Example:
"playing" might be divided into:
- play
- ing

Similarly, "unhappiness" might be divided into:
- un
- happi
- ness

These are illustrative examples. Actual divisions depend on the tokenizer.

Uses:
- Handling unfamiliar words
- Reducing vocabulary size
- Processing different forms of words

Subword tokenization is commonly used in modern language models.

### D. Tokens

Definition:
A token is a unit produced by a tokenizer. It may be a complete word, a subword, a character, punctuation, or a special symbol.

Example:
Text: "I love NLP"

A word-based tokenizer may produce:
["I", "love", "NLP"]

These are three tokens.

Another tokenizer might divide a word into multiple subword tokens.

Important:
A token is not always equal to a word. The number of tokens depends on the tokenizer.

### Comparison

| Unit | Example for "playing" |
|---|---|
| Characters | p, l, a, y, i, n, g |
| Word | playing |
| Subwords | play, ing |
| Tokens | Depends on the tokenizer |

## 3. Vocabulary

### Definition
A vocabulary is the collection of unique tokens recognized by an NLP system or tokenizer.

Consider these sentences:

1. "I love AI"
2. "I love NLP"
3. "AI is useful"

All word occurrences:
I, love, AI, I, love, NLP, AI, is, useful

Unique words:
I, love, AI, NLP, is, useful

Therefore, the vocabulary is:

["I", "love", "AI", "NLP", "is", "useful"]

Vocabulary size = 6

Total word occurrences = 9

### Vocabulary vs. Total Words

Vocabulary size counts each unique token only once.

Total word occurrences count every appearance of every token.

For example:

Sentence: "AI is useful and AI is powerful"

Tokens:
["AI", "is", "useful", "and", "AI", "is", "powerful"]

Total tokens = 7

Unique vocabulary:
["AI", "is", "useful", "and", "powerful"]

Vocabulary size = 5

### Vocabulary and Numerical IDs

Computers process numerical data, so tokens are often assigned numerical IDs.

Example:

| Token | Token ID |
|---|---:|
| I | 0 |
| love | 1 |
| AI | 2 |
| NLP | 3 |
| is | 4 |
| useful | 5 |

Sentence:
"I love AI"

Numerical representation:
[0, 1, 2]

This process maps text tokens to numbers.

The IDs above are illustrative. Real NLP models use their own vocabularies and token IDs.

### What Happens When a Word Is Unknown?

Suppose a model encounters a word that is not present in its vocabulary.

Depending on the tokenizer, the word may be:
- Divided into smaller subword tokens.
- Replaced with an unknown token such as [UNK].

Subword tokenization helps modern NLP models process many unfamiliar words.

## 4. Sequences and Sequence Length

### A. Sequence

Definition:
A sequence is an ordered collection of tokens.

Example:

Sentence:
"I love NLP"

Token sequence:
["I", "love", "NLP"]

The order of tokens matters because changing word order can change the meaning.

Example:
- Dog bites man.
- Man bites dog.

Both sentences contain the same words, but their meanings are different.

### B. Sequence Length

Definition:
Sequence length is the total number of tokens in a sequence.

Example:

Sentence:
"I love NLP"

Tokens:
["I", "love", "NLP"]

Sequence length = 3

More examples:

| Sentence | Tokens | Sequence Length |
|---|---|---:|
| Hello | ["Hello"] | 1 |
| I love AI | ["I", "love", "AI"] | 3 |
| I am learning NLP | ["I", "am", "learning", "NLP"] | 4 |
| AI is very useful | ["AI", "is", "very", "useful"] | 4 |

These examples use word-based tokenization. Other tokenizers may produce different sequence lengths.

### Why Is Sequence Length Important?

#### 1. Model Input Limits
Language models have limits on how many tokens they can process in a single context.

#### 2. Memory and Processing Time
Longer sequences generally require more memory and computation.

#### 3. Padding
When sequences have different lengths, shorter sequences can be padded to make them the same length.

Example:

Sequence 1: [I, run]
Sequence 2: [I, love, AI]
Sequence 3: [I, am, learning, NLP]

After padding:

[I, run, PAD, PAD]
[I, love, AI, PAD]
[I, am, learning, NLP]

PAD is a special token used to fill empty positions.

#### 4. Truncation
When a sequence is longer than the model's permitted input length, some tokens may need to be removed.

Example:

Original sequence:
[I, am, learning, natural, language, processing]

If the maximum length is 4:

[I, am, learning, natural]

The remaining tokens are truncated in this example.

## 5. Python Example

The following code demonstrates basic text preprocessing, tokenization, vocabulary creation, and sequence length.

```python
import re

text = "I love NLP! I love AI."

# 1. Convert to lowercase
cleanText = text.lower()

# 2. Remove punctuation
cleanText = re.sub(r"[^\w\s]", "", cleanText)

# 3. Tokenize the text
tokens = cleanText.split()

# 4. Create a vocabulary of unique tokens
vocabulary = sorted(set(tokens))

# 5. Calculate sequence length
sequenceLength = len(tokens)

# Display results
print("Original text:", text)
print("Cleaned text:", cleanText)
print("Tokens:", tokens)
print("Vocabulary:", vocabulary)
print("Vocabulary size:", len(vocabulary))
print("Sequence length:", sequenceLength)
